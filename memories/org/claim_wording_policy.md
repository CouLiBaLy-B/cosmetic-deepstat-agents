# Claim wording policy

## General principles

1. **Truthful and not misleading.** Every claim must be supported by the
   statistical evidence at the declared support level.
2. **Specific and verifiable.** Claims must reference the study population,
   duration, and measurement method.
3. **No absolute language** ("eliminates", "100%") unless the evidence is
   unambiguous.

## Templates by support level

### Confirmed (adjusted p < α, practical threshold met)

- "[Measurement] [direction] by [estimate] ([95% CI]) after [duration] of
  daily use, measured by [instrument] in [N] [population]."
- Example: "Skin hydration increased by +7.2 a.u. (95% CI: 5.1–9.3) after
  28 days of daily use, measured by Corneometer® in 30 women aged 40-60."

### Partial (significant but practical threshold not met)

- "A statistically significant [direction] of [estimate] was observed, but
  the predefined practical relevance threshold was not met."
- Marketing: downgrade to "helps [verb]" or "contributes to [noun]".

### Exploratory

- "Preliminary data suggest [observation]. Further study is needed."
- Marketing: NOT allowed in consumer-facing claims. Internal use only.

### Not supported

- "The study did not demonstrate a statistically significant [effect]."
- Marketing: no claim allowed.

## Consumer perception vs instrumental

- **Consumer:** "XX% of consumers reported [perception] (95% CI: [lo–hi%],
  N=[n])."
- **Instrumental:** "[Instrument] measurement showed [estimate] [unit]
  (p=[value], N=[n])."
- **Never mix** the two in a single claim sentence.

## Forbidden terms (all jurisdictions)

- "Medical", "treats", "cures", "therapeutic", "drug-like"
- "Anti-inflammatory" (unless OTC drug)
- "100% effective / safe / guaranteed"
- "Best in the world", "miracle"
