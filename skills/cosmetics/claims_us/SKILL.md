# US Cosmetic Claim Substantiation

## Regulatory framework

- **Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act)** — defines cosmetics
  as products intended to cleanse, beautify, promote attractiveness, or
  alter appearance **without affecting the body's structure or function**.
- **FDA Guidance for Industry** — if a product claims to affect structure
  or function (e.g. "stimulates collagen production"), it is a **drug**
  under the FD&C Act and requires a New Drug Application (NDA).
- **MoCRA (Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act, 2022)** — introduced
  mandatory adverse-event reporting, facility registration, and product
  listing for cosmetics.

## Cosmetic vs drug boundary

| Cosmetic claim (OK)                     | Drug claim (NOT OK for cosmetics)          |
|-----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| "Moisturises the skin"                  | "Treats eczema"                             |
| "Reduces the appearance of wrinkles"    | "Eliminates wrinkles"                       |
| "Cleanses"                             | "Anti-bacterial" (if therapeutic intent)     |
| "Helps skin look firmer"               | "Increases skin elasticity"                 |
| "Soothes"                              | "Anti-inflammatory"                          |
| "Appearance of …" (hedged)             | Any structure/function claim                |

**Key rule:** Use "appearance of" or "look of" language to stay on the
cosmetic side.

## Evidence requirements for US cosmetic claims

| Claim type           | Evidence needed                                |
|----------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Appearance claims    | Consumer perception study, instrumental optional |
| Moisturisation       | TEWL / Corneometer study, ≥ 20 subjects        |
| SPF claims           | FDA monograph testing (in vitro + in vivo)     |
| "Clinically tested"  | An actual clinical study (not just bench-top)  |
| "Dermatologist tested" | A dermatologist supervised the study          |
| Safety               | PCPC/CTFA safety evaluation, patch testing     |

## Wording rules

1. **Avoid absolute claims** ("100% effective").
2. **Hedge with "helps" or "appearance"** when the mechanism is cosmetic.
3. **Disclosure:** if using a consumer study, disclose panel size and
   methodology.
4. **No therapeutic claims** unless the product is registered as an
   OTC drug.
5. **Substantiation must be "competent and reliable scientific evidence"**
   per FTC standards.

## Differences from EU

| Aspect         | EU                                  | US                                   |
|----------------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Claim review   | Pre-market (Common Criteria)         | Post-market (FTC enforcement)        |
| Equivalence    | TOST with pre-specified margin       | Less formally defined                |
| AE reporting   | Cosmetics Regulation Art. 23         | MoCRA (2022), mandatory              |
| "Natural"      | ISO 16128 definitions encouraged     | No federal standard; FTC guidance    |
